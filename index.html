Workshop Summary

The convergence of large language models (LLMs) and multi-agent systems (MAS) has opened transformative opportunities across many domains, and biomedicine stands out as one of the most promising and impactful application areas. Biomedical research and healthcare are inherently complex, involving vast amounts of heterogeneous data—genomic sequences, medical images, electronic health records (EHRs), clinical notes, and scientific literature—that span multiple scales, modalities, and knowledge domains. No single AI model can capture the full breadth of expertise needed to navigate this complexity. Multi-agent systems, where multiple specialized AI agents collaborate, reason, and act in concert, offer a powerful paradigm to address these challenges by decomposing complex biomedical tasks into manageable subtasks handled by domain-specific agents.

In biomedical discovery, multi-agent systems have shown remarkable potential for accelerating scientific progress: AI agents can collaboratively mine literature to generate novel hypotheses, assist in drug discovery pipelines by coordinating molecular design with toxicity prediction and synthesis planning, and facilitate the analysis of large-scale omics data for identifying disease biomarkers and therapeutic targets. In healthcare, multi-agent frameworks are being explored for clinical decision support, where agents with expertise in different medical specialties can collectively analyze patient data to produce more accurate diagnoses, recommend personalized treatment plans, and monitor patient outcomes. These applications align naturally with the Big Data paradigm, as they require processing and reasoning over massive, distributed, and multi-modal datasets.

This workshop brings together researchers and practitioners from AI, biomedicine, and healthcare to discuss the latest advances, challenges, and future directions of multi-agent systems in biomedical applications. Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:
1. Multi-Agent Systems for Biomedical Research (literature mining, hypothesis generation, autonomous discovery)
2. Multi-Agent Systems for Disease Diagnosis and Clinical Decision Support
3. Multi-Agent Drug Discovery and Molecular Design
4. Agent-Based Systems for Genomics, Multi-Omics, and Medical Imaging
5. Safety, Reliability, and Trustworthiness of Biomedical AI Agents
6. Benchmarks, Evaluation, and Infrastructure for Biomedical Multi-Agent Systems

Call for Papers

The BioAgents workshop invites high-quality submissions on the design, deployment, evaluation, and societal implications of multi-agent systems for biomedical discovery and healthcare. We welcome contributions from researchers and practitioners in AI/ML, biomedical informatics, healthcare, and related disciplines.

Topics of Interest

We encourage submissions on topics including, but not limited to:

  • Multi-Agent Systems for Biomedical Research: LLM-based agents for literature mining, hypothesis generation, experiment design, retrieval-augmented generation, tool-augmented agents that interface with biomedical databases, and autonomous scientific discovery
  • Disease Diagnosis and Clinical Decision Support: Multi-agent consultation frameworks mimicking multidisciplinary team meetings, rare disease identification, multimodal clinical data integration, and personalized treatment planning
  • Drug Discovery and Molecular Design: Agents for target identification, lead optimization, ADMET prediction, synthesis planning, drug repurposing, and clinical trial simulation
  • Genomics, Multi-Omics, and Medical Imaging: Agents for genomic variant interpretation, multi-omics integration, spatial transcriptomics, computational pathology, and integrative diagnostic reporting
  • Safety, Reliability, and Trustworthiness: Hallucination detection and mitigation in medical contexts, factual grounding, uncertainty quantification, adversarial robustness, fairness auditing, and regulatory compliance (FDA, HIPAA, GDPR)
  • Benchmarks, Evaluation, and Infrastructure: Evaluation frameworks, simulation environments, federated multi-agent systems, and integration with biomedical knowledge graphs (UMLS, SNOMED CT, Gene Ontology)
  • Ethical, Regulatory, and Societal Aspects: Patient privacy, accountability, fairness across populations, and human-in-the-loop oversight for biomedical AI agents

Submission Guidelines

We invite two types of submissions:

  • Regular Papers: Up to 10 pages (IEEE Big Data format), presenting substantial and original contributions.
  • Short Papers: Up to 6 pages (IEEE Big Data format), presenting preliminary results, work-in-progress, or position papers.

All submissions must use the official IEEE Big Data 2026 LaTeX/Word style. Submissions will undergo single-blind peer review by at least three members of the program committee. Accepted papers will be included in the Workshop Proceedings published by the IEEE Computer Society Press.
